Communities & Villages

Gitata is made up of a core town and a wider belt of settlements and enclaves each with its own place in the community's traditional and agricultural life.

Central hubs & major administrative villages

Gitata town and its main districts.

Gitata Town

The central urban administrative seat and royal headquarters of the chiefdom.

Zango-Gitata

One of the largest and most populated commercial agrarian hubs in the district, known for its industrious local farmers.

Sabon Kasuwa Gitata

"New Market Gitata" the primary regional market centre where farmers aggregate crops for export to Abuja and Keffi.

Tudun Uku

A major residential, educational, and farming settlement close to the central district infrastructure.

Takalafiya

A peaceful agricultural village along the district's main commercial road corridor.

Buzi

A historically significant settlement where the Ogbom the native Gitata (Buzi) dialect of the Yeskwa language is spoken.

Dauro

A traditional farming village set toward the hilly terrain of Gitata's borders.

Traditional & agricultural enclaves

The wider belt of settlements.

Smaller communities and farming enclaves that make up Gitata's local culture and heritage.

Angwan Alura

A culturally vital Gwandara enclave known for preserving traditional folklore, community leadership structures, and ancestral festivals.

Kadeki

A strong indigenous community recognised within the chiefdom for its traditional dance displays during royal coronation ceremonies.

Marmara

A village within the Gitata network relying heavily on grain and root-crop farming distinct from the separate Marmara town in Nasarawa LGA.

Marma

A close sister settlement to Marmara, serving as a tight-knit family and farming outpost under Gitata's traditional authority.

Angwan Maisauri

An active agricultural community central to the district's aggregate yam and maize cultivation.

Angwan Mangoro

A progressive farming locality recognised in local educational zones for its agricultural land value.

Saniya

A quiet, outlying settlement officially mapped under the district's postal and electoral boundaries.

Ankara

An older, foundational farming homestead listed in state geographical registries under Gitata's jurisdiction.

Kutu

A residential enclave known for a harmonious blend of different ethnic families living and working together.

Saminaka

A community built around extended kinship ties, functioning as an outpost for regional crop trading.

Kyenpha

A smaller agrarian settlement of hardworking farmers focused on seasonal food production.

Angwan Chindo

A traditional Gwandara, Mada, and Fulani residential quarter on Gitata's agricultural outskirts, recognised regionally for its strong participation in smallholder animal husbandry. Households specialise in free-range poultry, livestock rearing, and root crops yams, beans, maize, and cassava supplying the Karu urban area.

Angwan Chada

A sister community neighbouring Angwan Alura, functioning as a rural farming cluster established by indigenous settlers. Bounded by arable land, families here practise seasonal bush-fallow farming focused on food-security crops maize, guinea corn, and local beans.

Angwan Dadi

A traditional ward within Gitata's farming belt full detail on this settlement is still to be confirmed.

Angwan Mallam

A ward whose name points to a historical association with a Mallam (Islamic scholar/teacher), suggesting early roots in religious or educational life full detail still to be confirmed.

Danko

A settlement within Gitata's agrarian belt full detail still to be confirmed.

Angwan Giwa

"Giwa" in the ward's name may reference a local landmark, historical event, or founding figure full detail still to be confirmed.

Angwan Madaki

Its name echoes the Madawaki title, a border-defence role in Gitata's traditional cabinet, hinting at a historical link to the district's defensive structure

Angwan Maiyaki

"Maiyaki"suggests ties to Gitata's Sarkin Yaki lineage or a history connected to defence full detail still to be confirmed.

Angwan Tsakiya

"Tsakiya"suggests this ward sits centrally within Gitata's settlement layout full detail still to be confirmed.

Kotorshi

A settlement within Gitata's traditional belt full detail still to be confirmed.

Rafin Taba

A historic settlement built alongside one of the natural valley water channels the Hausa name translates to "Tobacco Stream," reflecting the historic cultivation of tobacco along the wet riverbanks. Today the stream supports dry-season irrigation, letting farmers grow green vegetables, peppers, and sugarcane after the rains stop.

Goska

A quiet, low-density agrarian village nestled along Tudun Uku distinct from the larger, conflict-impacted town of the same name elsewhere in the region; this Gitata outpost is entirely peaceful. It's a hub for local crop trading, timber harvesting, and small-scale charcoal production, relying on Gitata Central for healthcare and market days.
