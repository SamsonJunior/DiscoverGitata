History & Heritage

The record of how Gitata came to be — kept the way a chronicle should be: dated, sourced, and added to over time.

The ledger

A timeline, not a textbook.

Gitata's story is carried through the Gwandara migration out of Kano, the Yeskwa settlement of its hills, and a name reshaped by a colonial road-builder's ear.

c. 1470 – 1500 AD

Exodus from Kano

Under the reign of Emir Muhammadu Rumfa, Islam was strictly institutionalised at the Kano court and traditional practice pushed out. A traditionalist leader, Karshi, refused to abandon his ancestors' religion — the worship of spirits such as Tsumburbura through sacred dance rituals atop Kano's Dala hills. Rather than convert, he led his people south into exile. Their choice earned them the name Gwanda-rawa da sallah — "better to dance than to pray" — later shortened to Gwandara.

Migration south

Founding the middle-belt towns

Travelling down through Zaria and Abuja, the Gwandara settled the plains of Keffi and Karu, founding a string of towns that included Karu, Keffi, Panda — and Gitata.

Late 17th century

The Yeskwa (Nyankpa) arrive

The Yeskwa people migrated from Darigo, near the present-day Kaduna–Nasarawa boundary, and settled in the hills around Gitata seeking refuge. They lived alongside the Gwandara peacefully, intermarrying and trading, and together the two groups shaped the cultural identity Gitata carries today.

Uphill, then down

Gidzi-atata — a house on the plain

With slave raids and tribal conflict common across the region, the ancestors first built on high ground for safety — what they called Gidzi-atata. Once the danger eased, they descended to settle permanently on the plain below.

Colonial era

Where the name "Gitata" comes from

During construction of the colonial road linking Keffi and Kaduna, officials asked the settlement's name. Gidzi-atata was misheard and recorded as Gitata — the name that has stuck ever since.

Present

Gitata today

Gitata now sits within Karu Local Government Area, Nasarawa State part of the fast-growing Karu–Mararaba–Nyanya corridor bordering Abuja, still carrying the record of the peoples who founded it.
